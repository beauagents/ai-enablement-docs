## Summary

The page presents the FAIR Guiding Principles as guidance for improving the Findability, Accessibility, Interoperability, and Reusability of digital resources. It states that metadata and data should be easy to find for humans and computers, and that machine-readable metadata are essential for automatic discovery. The principles cover findable, accessible, interoperable, and reusable data and metadata, including identifiers, metadata, protocols, vocabularies, references, licenses, provenance, and community standards. The page also references the 2016 article "The FAIR Guiding Principles for scientific data management and stewardship."

## Parsed evidence

- F1 requires (meta)data to be assigned a globally unique and persistent identifier. ^ev-1
- F2 requires data to be described with rich metadata, and F3 requires metadata to explicitly include the identifier of the data they describe. ^ev-2
- A1 requires (meta)data to be retrievable by identifier using a standardised communications protocol; A1.1 and A1.2 address open implementation and authentication or authorisation. ^ev-3
- I1–I3 require formal and broadly applicable knowledge-representation languages, FAIR vocabularies, and qualified references to other (meta)data. ^ev-4
- R1–R1.3 address rich and accurate attributes, clear data usage licenses, detailed provenance, and domain-relevant community standards. ^ev-5
- The reference is: "The FAIR Guiding Principles for scientific data management and stewardship," Sci Data 3, 160018 (2016), DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/sdata.2016.18. ^ev-6
