## Summary

The OECD AI Principles promote innovative and trustworthy AI that respects human rights and democratic values. They guide AI actors in developing trustworthy AI and provide policymakers with recommendations for effective AI policies. The Principles include five values-based principles and five recommendations for policymakers. There are currently 47 adherents to the Principles.

## Parsed evidence

- The OECD AI Principles were initially adopted in 2019 and updated in May 2024. ^ev-1
- The five values-based principles cover inclusive growth, sustainable development and well-being; human rights and democratic values, including fairness and privacy; transparency and explainability; robustness, security and safety; and accountability. ^ev-2
- The recommendations for policymakers cover AI research and development, an inclusive AI-enabling ecosystem, an interoperable governance and policy environment, human capacity and labour market transition, and international co-operation. ^ev-3
- The OECD Recommendation on AI is described as the first intergovernmental standard on AI. ^ev-4
- There are 47 adherents to the AI Principles. ^ev-5
- An AI system is defined as a machine-based system that infers how to generate outputs such as predictions, content, recommendations, or decisions that can influence physical or virtual environments. ^ev-6
